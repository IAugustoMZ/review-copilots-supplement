# Methodology Decision Journal

Chronological record of every methodological decision taken while rebuilding and
extending this review's corpus, written as each decision was made rather than
reconstructed afterwards. It exists so the manuscript's methods section can be
assembled from a contemporaneous log, and so a reader (or reviewer) can trace
any corpus number back to the decision that produced it.

**How to read this file.** Each entry records *what* was decided, *why*, and
*what it constrains* — the last matters most, because several of these decisions
limit what the review is entitled to claim. Entries marked **⚠** carry a
limitation the manuscript must state explicitly rather than bury.

Full narrative and counts live in the two topical documents; this journal is the
index and the chronology:

- `SEARCH_AND_SNOWBALLING.md` — search strategy, snowballing, saturation, persistence
- `SAMPLING_AND_AGREEMENT.md` — validation sample, Cohen's κ, stopping rule

| D-31 | **The assisted classifier's per-category probabilities are Platt-calibrated, not softmax-normalised, and a category needs 8 positives before it is fit at all.** The author proposed adding post-prediction softmax normalisation across each dimension's one-vs-all category probabilities. The one-vs-all structure was already in place (`_fit_dimension_category_classifiers`: one binary presence/absence classifier per category), but softmax was rejected on three grounds. (1) Five of the six dimensions are **multi-label** -- a paper can be in two domains at once -- so forcing the row to sum to 1 turns co-occurring categories into competing ones: two genuine 0.9s become ~0.5 each and both fall out of the `high` bucket. (2) `bucket_suggestion` applies **absolute** cutoffs (0.70/0.40/0.15); softmax makes each probability relative to how many categories the dimension defines, so in `application` (13 categories) the `high` bucket would be permanently empty and the cutoffs would have to be retuned per dimension, arbitrarily. (3) It breaks monotonicity across records -- a paper's `domain=X` probability would depend on its scores for the other six categories. `evidence_type`, the one single-value dimension, is already a single multinomial fit whose `predict_proba` sums to 1, so softmax there is a no-op at best. **The real problem behind the request is genuine**: independently-fit one-vs-all probabilities are not comparable across categories (a category with 9 positives and one with 120 score on different effective scales, and `class_weight="balanced"` deliberately pulls both off the true base rate), which is exactly what absolute cutoffs require. The fix is calibration, not normalisation: `_fit_calibrated_binary` fits a two-parameter sigmoid on the log-odds of each category's **out-of-fold** scores -- the same out-of-fold pass the function already ran, switched from `method="predict"` to `method="predict_proba"`, so the added cost is one 1-D logistic fit and no nested CV (sklearn's `CalibratedClassifierCV(cv=k)` would have multiplied the fit count by k across dozens of categories x 8 model types and turned a per-batch retrain into minutes). After calibration 0.70 means "~70% of papers scoring here are in this category", per category, independently, with categories left uncoupled. Two documented approximations, both standard and both shared with `CalibratedClassifierCV(ensemble=False)`: the base model is refit on all data while the calibrator describes fold models, and the returned out-of-fold labels are thresholded from probabilities the calibrator itself saw. **Reported dimension metrics will move**, for a real reason: the old labels came from `estimator.predict()`, whose argmax sits on top of `class_weight="balanced"` and over-calls positives for rare categories; thresholding calibrated probabilities restores the base rate, so rare categories show lower recall and higher precision. Separately, `MIN_POSITIVES_PER_CATEGORY = 8` now governs the positive side (`MIN_EXAMPLES_PER_CLASS = 2` still governs the negative side and every single-value fit): a category with three positives is fittable and calibratable, and that is the hazard -- it would hand the reviewer a confident number backed by three papers. Under the bar the category makes no suggestion and stays on the manual option list, so early batches suggest only the categories that have accumulated evidence. | this file |

Under D-36's marginals κ_max = 0.164, so that bound can never clear the floor: every batch would have printed "stopping rule not met yet", indefinitely, for a reason that has nothing to do with how well anyone is judging records. Found while wiring D-39, and it matters immediately because the next 100 adjudications will run under this panel. The panel now prints κ_max alongside κ (with κ as a percentage of its ceiling), plus AC1 and AUC, and when the ceiling sits at or below the floor it says so explicitly — "kappa cannot exceed X no matter how the remaining records are judged" — and falls back to stopping on **precision alone** (half-width ≤ 0.15). The constant is kept rather than lowered: an arbitrary floor tuned until it passes is worse than a floor stated and shown to be unreachable. | this file |

## Screening rules as applied (Phase 10)

These are the operational readings of `config/screening_criteria.yaml` that the
screening actually used. They are recorded because two analysts applying the
written criteria could otherwise diverge on exactly these boundaries.

**Included**
- LLM/foundation-model/agentic systems assisting an engineering task: process
  simulation copilots, flowsheet generation, incident root-cause classification,
  operating-procedure generation, process-safety foundation models.
- Enabling-technology work when it grounds RQ1: surveys of generative
  transformers, diffusion models, multimodal foundation models, LLM-enhanced RL.
- Capability evidence for the domain: chemistry benchmarks of LLMs (ChemBench,
  ChemIQ, eight-task benchmarks), because they measure what a copilot would rely on.
- Benchmarks, evaluation environments and training-data construction **in
  industrial/process/engineering contexts** (from D-19).
- Cross-domain engineering applications as RQ2/RQ3 comparison context: LLMs in
  mechanical engineering, building energy, life-cycle assessment, systems
  engineering.

**Excluded**
- `wrong_technology` — classical ML/RL/PINN/MPC/surrogates/Bayesian optimization
  in process engineering, with no generative component (D-15).
- `off_topic` — education and pedagogy (including chemical-engineering teaching
  with LLMs), medicine, finance, banking, telecoms, policy and governance,
  information science; molecular and materials generative design (D-16); drug
  discovery.
- `not_original_research` — commentaries and personal perspectives.
- `benchmark_other_domain` — coding, web-browsing and general-reasoning agent
  benchmarks (D-20).

**Uncertain** is used, not avoided: it is reserved for records where the abstract
does not settle whether the system is generative, and for title-only records
whose scope cannot be read from the title. AI screening structurally cannot set
a final decision, so `uncertain` defers to human adjudication rather than
silently discarding a record.

⚠ **Title-only screening.** 1,569 of 7,794 D1 records (20%) have no abstract and
were screened on title alone, at reduced confidence. This subgroup will tend to
depress κ through information limits rather than genuine criterion disagreement,
which is an argument for stratifying the validation sample by abstract presence.
